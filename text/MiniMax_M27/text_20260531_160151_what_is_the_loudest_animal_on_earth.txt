The animal that holds the record for the **loudest sound produced by a living creature** is the **sperm whale** (​*Physeter macrocephalus*).

| Animal | Typical loudness (source level) | Where the sound is measured |
|--------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Sperm whale** | **≈ 230 dB re 1 µPa at 1 m** (some clicks measured up to ~236 dB) | Underwater (the sea) |
| **Blue whale** | ~188 dB re 1 µPa at 1 m | Underwater |
| **Howler monkey** (loudest land animal) | ~140 dB re 20 µPa at 1 m (≈ 140 dB SPL in air) | Air |
| **Pistol (snapping) shrimp** | ~200 dB re 1 µPa at 1 m (cavitation “snap”) | Underwater (but it’s a mechanical bubble collapse, not a vocalization) |
| **Cicada** (loudest insect) | up to ~120 dB SPL at 1 m | Air |

### Why the sperm whale wins

- **Click sound**: Sperm whales produce powerful clicks used for echolocation and communication. Those clicks are brief, broadband pulses that can travel hundreds of kilometres in the ocean.
- **Energy concentration**: The sound is generated by forcing air through a specialized organ called the “phonic lips” in the forehead, creating a pressure wave of extraordinary intensity.
- **Measurement context**: In water, decibels are referenced to a micro‑Pascal (µPa) at 1 m. Because the acoustic impedance of water is much higher than that of air, a comparable “loudness” in air would be far higher in dB SPL, but we compare underwater source levels when ranking animals overall.

### Quick comparison

- **Sperm whale click** ≈ 230 dB (underwater).  
- **Blue whale “song”** ≈ 188 dB (underwater).  
- **Howler monkey roar** ≈ 140 dB (air).  

Thus, when the question is asked in a general sense—*the loudest animal on Earth*—the answer is the **sperm whale**, whose clicks are the most intense animal‑generated sounds ever recorded. If the question is narrowed to *loudest land animal*, the title goes to the **howler monkey**.
