The deepest lake in the world is **Lake Baikal** in Siberia, Russia.  

- **Maximum depth:** ≈ 1,642 meters (5,387 feet)  
- **Volume:** ≈ 23,615 km³, which is about 20 % of the world’s unfrozen fresh surface water  
- **Age:** About 25–30 million years old, making it also one of the oldest lakes on Earth  
- **Notable features:** It holds an extraordinary array of endemic species (e.g., the Baikal seal, numerous fish, and mollusks) and contains more than 1,000 m of sediment in its basin.

Other extremely deep lakes include Lake Tanganyika (≈ 1,470 m) and Lake Vostok (≈ 900 m, but it lies beneath Antarctic ice), but none surpass Lake Baikal’s depth.
