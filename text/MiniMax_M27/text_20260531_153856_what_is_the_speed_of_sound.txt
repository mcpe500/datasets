The speed of sound is the distance a pressure wave travels per unit time in a given medium.  The most commonly quoted value is for **dry air at sea‑level and 20 °C (68 °F)**, where:

| Quantity | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Meters per second (m s⁻¹)** | **≈ 343 m/s** |
| **Kilometres per hour (km/h)** | **≈ 1235 km/h** |
| **Miles per hour (mph)** | **≈ 767 mph** |
| **Mach 1** | This is the reference (Mach 1 ≈ 343 m/s in those conditions) |

### Why it varies
- **Temperature**: The speed rises with temperature because the molecules move faster. In cold air at 0 °C the speed is about 331 m/s; at 30 °C it’s about 349 m/s.
- **Humidity**: Moist air is less dense than dry air, so sound actually travels a little faster in humid conditions (≈ 0.1 % faster at typical humidity).
- **Pressure / altitude**: In an ideal gas at a given temperature, pressure has a negligible direct effect; the dominant altitude effect comes from temperature changes in the atmosphere.
- **Medium**: Different materials support sound at very different speeds.  

| Medium | Approx. speed (m/s) |
|--------|-------------------|
| Air (20 °C, sea‑level) | **≈ 343** |
| Fresh water (25 °C) | **≈ 1498** |
| Seawater (25 °C) | **≈ 1531** |
| Steel (bulk) | **≈ 5960** |
| Aluminum | **≈ 6420** |
| Diamond | **≈ 12000** |

### Quick formula for dry air
For an ideal gas, the speed of sound can be expressed as  

\[
c = \sqrt{\gamma \, R \, T / M},
\]

where  

- \(\gamma\) = ratio of specific heats (≈ 1.40 for diatomic air)  
- \(R\) = universal gas constant (8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹)  
- \(T\) = absolute temperature (K)  
- \(M\) = molar mass of air (≈ 0.028964 kg mol⁻¹).

Plugging in \(T = 293 K\) (20 °C) yields ≈ 343 m/s.

### Bottom line
- **In everyday conditions (dry air, ~20 °C, sea level)** the speed of sound is **≈ 343 m/s** (≈ 767 mph).
