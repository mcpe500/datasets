Here are four commonly‑recognized vertical zones of the open ocean:

1. **Epipelagic Zone (Sunlit Zone)** – Extends from the surface down to about 200 m. It receives abundant sunlight, supports photosynthesis, and contains the majority of marine life.

2. **Mesopelagic Zone (Twilight Zone)** – Lies roughly between 200 m and 1,000 m. Light is dim but still present; many organisms have adaptations like large eyes or bioluminescence.

3. **Bathypelagic Zone (Midnight Zone)** – From about 1,000 m to 4,000 m. No sunlight penetrates; temperatures are cold and pressure high. Life here is sparse but includes lanternfish, squid, and some deep‑sea mammals.

4. **Abyssopelagic Zone (Abyssal Zone)** – Extends from 4,000 m to the ocean floor at ~6,000 m. Extremely cold, near‑freezing temperatures, and immense pressure. Organisms are mostly filter‑feeders, sea stars, and deep‑sea fish adapted to this darkness.

(If you also include the deepest trenches, the **Hadopelagic Zone**—the hadal zone—lies below 6,000 m, but the four zones above are the most commonly referenced.)
