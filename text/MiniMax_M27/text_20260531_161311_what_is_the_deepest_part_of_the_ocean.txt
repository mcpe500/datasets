The deepest known point in the ocean is **the Challenger Deep**, which lies at the southern end of the **Mariana Trench** in the western Pacific Ocean.

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Location** | Mariana Trench, about 300 km (190 mi) southwest of Guam |
| **Depth** | Approximately **10,984 meters (36,070 feet)** (most recent measurements) |
| **Pressure** | >1,000 times atmospheric pressure at sea level (≈108 MPa) |
| **Temperature** | A few degrees above freezing (≈1–4 °C) |
| **First human visit** | Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh in the bathyscaphe *Trieste* (1960) |
| **Recent solo descent** | Film‑director James Cameron in the Deepsea Challenger (2012) |
| **Robotic survey** | The Five Deeps Expedition (2020) used an autonomous submersible to confirm the depth with high‑resolution multibeam sonar |

### Why it’s the deepest

- The **Mariana Trench** is a subduction zone where the Pacific Plate slides beneath the smaller Mariana Plate, creating a narrow, extremely deep trench.
- The **Challenger Deep** sits at the southernmost tip of this trench, where the ocean floor reaches its maximum depth.

### How the depth is measured

Modern depth measurements rely on **multibeam sonar** and **autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs)** that emit sound pulses and record the time it takes for the echo to return. Recent expeditions have refined the earlier estimate of ~11,000 m to the more precise figure of ~10,984 m (± a few meters).

### Quick facts for context

- **Depth in miles:** ≈ 6.83 mi (about 11 km).
- **Comparable height:** If Mount Everest (8,849 m) were placed into the Challenger Deep, its summit would still be about **2,135 m (7,000 ft)** below the surface.
- **Exploration:** Only a handful of crewed and uncrewed vehicles have reached the bottom, making it one of the least‑explored environments on Earth.

So, when someone asks “What is the deepest part of the ocean?” the answer is **the Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench**, with a depth of roughly **11 km (36,000 ft)**.
