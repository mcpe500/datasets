A rainbow isn’t a physical object that has a “finish line” you can walk up to, so you can’t really find anything at its literal end. What you **do** find depends on whether you’re looking through a scientific lens or a folklore one.

---

## 1. The scientific view – there is no “end”

| What a rainbow actually is | Why there’s no endpoint |
|----------------------------|------------------------|
| A set of light‑refracting and reflecting events inside millions of tiny water droplets. | Each droplet produces a cone of light at a fixed angle (≈ 42° for the primary rainbow). When you look at the sky, your eyes see the overlapping contributions of all the droplets that lie along that cone. |
| A circular arc (or full circle if you’re high enough) centered on the point opposite the Sun (the anti‑solar point). | The arc you see is an *apparent* shape formed by the intersection of that cone with the sky. It follows you as you move, so the “closest” point to you is always at about that same angular distance from the anti‑solar direction. |
| Unique to each observer (and even to each eye). | Because the rainbow is defined by the geometry of the light, the exact rainbow you see belongs to your own line of sight. Two people standing side‑by‑side will see slightly different rainbows, each with its own “end” that moves with them. |

In short, the “end” of a rainbow is just a point in the sky where the colored arc dips closest to the horizon. If you tried to walk toward it, it would stay the same angular distance away and never get any closer—hence the classic saying that you can’t catch a rainbow.

---

## 2. The folklore view – a pot of gold (and maybe a leprechaun)

| Mythic tradition | What it says you’ll find |
|------------------|--------------------------|
| Irish legend & many children’s stories | **A hidden pot of gold** guarded by a **leprechaun**. The idea is that if you reach the exact spot where the rainbow touches the ground, you’ll uncover a treasure. |
| Why the legend persists | It’s a fun metaphor for the idea that the most precious rewards lie at the end of something beautiful but seemingly unreachable. It also gives a playful incentive to chase “rainbow’s ends” – a harmless game of imagination. |

So, in a story‑book sense, the end of a rainbow is a **leprechaun’s pot of gold** (plus the inevitable trickster waiting to outwit you). In reality, you’ll just get wet if you wander into the rain that created the rainbow.

---

## 3. What you *might* actually see at the “foot” of a rainbow

Even though there’s no literal treasure, you can still find some interesting things at the spot where the rainbow appears to meet the ground:

| Possible “foot” location | What you might encounter |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Open field, meadow, or park** on a rainy day when the Sun breaks through. | Wet grass, maybe a few puddles, and a great view of the colorful arc overhead. |
| **Urban rooftop or hillside** on a clear‑after‑rain day. | A striking contrast between the bright rainbow and the cityscape or landscape below. |
| **A quiet lake or pond** reflecting the sky. | The rainbow can be mirrored in the water, doubling the visual spectacle. |
| **A leprechaun‑themed festival** (purely for fun). | Costumes, gold‑painted trinkets, and a playful treasure hunt that mimics the myth. |

---

## 4. TL;DR – The bottom line

- **Science:** No physical endpoint. A rainbow is an optical phenomenon created by light interacting with raindrops; each observer sees a personal, moving arc.
- **Folklore:** The classic “pot of gold” guarded by a leprechaun lives at the imagined end.
- **Reality:** At the place where the rainbow seems to touch the earth you’ll most likely just see rain‑soaked ground, a puddle, or a scenic view—unless you’ve arranged a playful treasure hunt for friends.

If you’re after the gold, you’ll have more luck setting up a hidden treasure in your backyard and pretending the rainbow points to it than waiting for a genuine leprechaun to appear! 🌈🍀
