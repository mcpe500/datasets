The river most commonly cited as the longest in the world is the **Nile River** in Africa.  

| River | Approximate length* |
|-------|----------------------|
| **Nile** | ~6,650 km (≈4,130 mi) |
| **Amazon** (South America) | ~6,400 km (≈4,000 mi) – though some recent measurements place it slightly longer (up to ~6,800 km) |

### Why the Nile is usually called the longest
- **Traditional source‑to‑mouth measurement** starts at Lake Victoria’s tributary in Burundi (the Kagera River) and ends at the Mediterranean Sea in Egypt.
- Most standard reference works (e.g., the *Encyclopedia Britannica*, the *U.S. Geological Survey*) list the Nile at about 6,650 km.

### The Amazon debate
- Recent studies, using satellite data and field surveys, have identified a longer headwater for the Amazon (often the Mantaro River in Peru) and argue that, when measured from that source, the Amazon can exceed 7,000 km, potentially making it the longest.
- Because the Amazon’s exact source is still debated and the measurement conventions differ, many authorities still list the Nile as the longest.

### Quick facts
- **Nile** drains about 3.3 million km², flowing through 11 countries (Burundi, Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, Eritrea, South Sudan, Sudan, Egypt, and the partially recognized state of South Sudan).
- **Amazon** carries the greatest volume of water (≈209,000 m³/s at its mouth) and drains about 7 million km² across nine countries (Brazil, Peru, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Bolivia, Guyana, Suriname, and French Guiana).

**Bottom line:** By the most widely accepted conventional measurements, the **Nile** holds the title of the world’s longest river, but the **Amazon** is a close contender and, according to some recent research, may actually be longer.
