**The sailfish is generally regarded as the fastest sea creature.**  

| Animal | Maximum recorded speed* | Scientific name |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------|
| Sailfish (Indo‑Pacific) | **≈ 68 mph (≈ 110 km/h)** | *Istiophorus platypterus* |
| Black marlin (sometimes cited) | ≈ 80 mph (≈ 130 km/h) | *Istiompax indica* |
| Swordfish | ≈ 60 mph (≈ 97 km/h) | *Xiphias gladius* |
| Common dolphin (bottlenose) | ≈ 34 mph (≈ 55 km/h) | *Tursiops truncatus* |
| Yellowfin tuna | ≈ 45 mph (≈ 72 km/h) | *Thunnus albacares* |

\*Speeds are **burst** measurements (short sprints) rather than cruising speeds, because getting an accurate continuous speed for a wild fish is very difficult.  

### Why the sailfish tops the list
- **Hydrodynamic shape:** Its long, slender body, stiff pectoral and dorsal fins, and a “bill” that cuts water reduce drag.
- **Muscle power:** A high proportion of fast‑twitch muscle fibers lets it generate rapid bursts of thrust.
- **Speed record:** Scientific studies and sport‑fishing observations have recorded sailfish swimming at up to 110 km/h in short dashes.

### A note on the “black marlin” claim
Some sources claim black marlin can reach ~130 km/h (80 mph). Those figures come from limited observations (e.g., a single tagged fish) and are less consistently reproduced than the sailfish data. Most marine‑biology textbooks and fisheries agencies still list the sailfish as the fastest fish.

### Quick fun facts about the sailfish
- **Size:** Typically 2.5–3.5 m (8–11 ft) long; the record is about 3.7 m (12 ft).
- **Habitat:** Found in tropical and subtropical oceans worldwide, often near the surface.
- **Behavior:** Uses its bill to herd schools of small fish, then slams them for a quick meal.
- **Conservation:** Listed as “Least Concern” by the IUCN, but they’re heavily targeted by sport fisheries, so sustainable practices matter.

So, if you’re looking for the ocean’s speed champion, the **sailfish** takes the crown (with the black marlin a close runner‑up).
