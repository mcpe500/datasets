**Short answer**

- **In a stoop (diving‑flight) the peregrine falcon (*Falco peregrinus*) is the fastest bird** – it has been recorded at ≈ 300 km h⁻¹ (≈ 186 mph).  
- **In level (horizontal) flight the common swift (*Apus apus*) holds the record for the highest sustained speed** – about 105 km h⁻¹ (≈ 65 mph).  

---

### Why the distinction matters

| Flight mode | Typical speed | Representative species |
|-------------|----------------|--------------------------|
| **Level (horizontal) flight** – the bird’s forward speed while cruising or migrating. | 70–120 km h⁻¹ (≈ 44–75 mph) for the fastest species. | Common swift, white‑throated needletail (≈ 110 km h⁻¹), red‑breasted merganser (≈ 130 km h⁻¹). |
| **Stoop (diving) flight** – a rapid vertical or steep dive used for hunting. | 200–350 km h⁻¹ (≈ 125–220 mph). | Peregrine falcon (≈ 300 km h⁻¹), gyrfalcon (≈ 200 km h⁻¹). |

The “fastest flying bird” is therefore a matter of which flight mode you’re interested in.

---

### Fast birds in level flight (selected examples)

| Species | Max recorded speed (km h⁻¹) | Notes |
|---------|----------------------------|-------|
| **Common swift** (*Apus apus*) | 105 | Often cited as the “fastest level‑flight bird” in the Guinness World Records. |
| **White‑throated needletail** (*Aerodramus spp.*) | 110–120 | Also called the “spine‑tailed swift”; occasional reports up to 170 km h⁻¹, but those figures are less reliably measured. |
| **Red‑breasted merganser** (*Mergus serrator*) | 129 | Recorded in a wind‑tunnel study; sometimes listed as the fastest horizontal speed for a duck. |
| **Eurasian hobby** (*Falco subbuteo*) | 115 | Small falcon that can keep up with swifts while chasing insects. |
| **Swallow‑tailed kite** (*Elanoides forficatus*) | 110 | Long, pointed wings give it high cruising speeds. |

*All of these are “fast” but still well below the dive speeds of falcons.*

---

### Fast birds in a stoop (diving)

| Species | Recorded dive speed (km h⁻¹) | Context |
|---------|------------------------------|---------|
| **Peregrine falcon** (*Falco peregrinus*) | 300–320 | Measured via radar/ GPS telemetry during hunting stoops; can exceed 350 km h⁻¹ in extreme cases. |
| **Gyrfalcon** (*Falco rusticolus*) | 200–250 | Uses steep dives over open tundra. |
| **Saker falcon** (*Falco cherrug*) | 180–210 | Occasionally recorded close to the peregrine’s performance. |
| **Golden eagle** (*Aquila chrysaetos*) | 150–200 | Dive speeds during territorial displays. |

The peregrine’s “stoop” is a textbook example of a bird converting gravitational energy into kinetic energy, achieving the highest known velocities in the animal kingdom.

### Bottom line

- **If you’re asking about the fastest *dive*:** the **peregrine falcon** (≈ 300 km h⁻¹).  
- **If you’re asking about the fastest *horizontal* flight:** the **common swift** (≈ 105 km h⁻¹).  

Both are remarkable aviators, but they achieve their records in very different ways.
