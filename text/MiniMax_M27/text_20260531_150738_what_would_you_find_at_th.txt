The classic folklore answer is “a pot of gold” guarded by a leprechaun. In Irish legend, the rainbow’s endpoint is where a sneaky little elf hides his treasure, and the only way to claim it is to outwit the leprechaun.

From a scientific point of view, however, there’s no literal “end” you can walk to. A rainbow is an optical phenomenon caused by the refraction, reflection, and dispersion of sunlight inside countless water droplets. Each observer sees their own personal rainbow, and the apparent “spot” where the rainbow appears follows you as you move—its location depends on the angle between the sun, the droplets, and your eyes. So while the legend says “pot of gold,” in reality you’ll only ever find more rain‑spattered sky. 

But that’s part of the charm of a rainbow: it invites both myth‑making and wonder, reminding us that sometimes the most alluring treasure is the beauty of the phenomenon itself. If you’re after something more tangible, the best place to look is a field after a summer storm, where you’ll find a spectacular, fleeting rainbow waiting to be chased (or photographed) rather than a leprechaun’s loot.
