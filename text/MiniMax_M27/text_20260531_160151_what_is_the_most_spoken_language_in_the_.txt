**Short answer:**  
- **If you count only native speakers**, Mandarin Chinese is the world’s most‑spoken language.  
- **If you count all speakers (native + second‑language)**, English comes out on top.

---

### Why the distinction matters

| Language | Approx. native speakers | Approx. total speakers (native + L2) | Source |
|----------|------------------------|--------------------------------------|--------|
| **Mandarin Chinese** | **~1.1 billion** | **~1.3 billion** | Ethnologue (2023) |
| **English** | **~400 million** | **~1.5 billion** | Ethnologue (2023) |
| **Hindi** | **~600 million** | **~900 million** | Ethnologue (2023) |
| **Spanish** | **~540 million** | **~580 million** | Ethnologue (2023) |

*The “total‑speaker” numbers are estimates because they depend on how people define “knowing” a language (e.g., basic conversation vs. full fluency).*

---

### Quick context

- **Mandarin Chinese** is the official language of mainland China, Taiwan, Singapore, and one of the official languages of the United Nations. Its huge native‑speaker base comes from the massive population of mainland China.
- **English** is the dominant language of international business, science, aviation, entertainment, and the internet. It is taught as a second language (ESL) in schools worldwide, which inflates its total‑speaker count dramatically.

---

### Bottom line

- **Native speakers:** Mandarin Chinese wins.
- **Total people who can speak the language (including second‑language speakers):** English wins.

So the “most spoken language” depends on how you count, but those are the two leading contenders.
