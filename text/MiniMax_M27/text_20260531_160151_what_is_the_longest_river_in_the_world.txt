The river most commonly cited as the longest in the world is the **Nile** (Arabic: النيل, Ancient Egyptian: Ḥꜥpī).  

| River | Approx. length* | Primary countries it flows through |
|-------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Nile** | 6,650 km (≈4,130 mi) | Uganda, South Sudan, Sudan, Egypt, Ethiopia, Kenya, DR Congo, Tanzania |
| **Amazon** | 6,400 km (≈4,000 mi) | Peru, Colombia, Brazil, Bolivia, Ecuador, Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname |

\*Lengths are approximate because they depend on how the river’s source and mouth are defined and on seasonal variations.

### Why the Nile is usually called the “longest”

- **Historical consensus:** Since the 19th century, cartographers have measured the Nile from its most distant recognized source (usually the **Ruvyironza** or **Kagera River** in Burundi) to its delta on the Mediterranean Sea.
- **Official measurements:** Many geographic and governmental sources list the Nile at about 6,650 km (4,130 mi).

### The Amazon’s claim

- Some recent studies (e.g., a 2007 AP report and a 2019 Brazilian survey) argue that the Amazon’s true length, when the most distant source in the **Mantaro River** in Peru is included, may be **≈7,000 km**, surpassing the Nile.
- The measurement debate hinges on whether to count seasonal tributaries and how the river’s mouth (whether the estuary or a particular channel) is defined.

### Bottom line

- **Traditional answer:** The **Nile** is the longest river, at roughly 6,650 km.
- **Contested answer:** Some modern surveys suggest the **Amazon** may be longer, but the Nile still holds the widely‑accepted title in most textbooks and reference works.
