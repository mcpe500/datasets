The deepest known point in the world’s oceans is **the Challenger Deep**, located in the **Mariana Trench** in the western Pacific Ocean, near the island of Guam.

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Name** | Challenger Deep |
| **Location** | Mariana Trench, western Pacific (≈11°21′ N 142°12′ E) |
| **Depth** | ≈ 10,925 m (≈ 35,843 ft). Recent high‑precision surveys (2020) give a best estimate of **10,984 m ± 25 m** (≈ 36,070 ft). |
| **Pressure** | ~108.6 MPa (≈ 1,071  atmospheres) |
| **First measurement** | The HMS *Challenger* expedition (1951) first identified a “deep” in this area; the exact depth was refined later by sonar and submersible dives. |
| **Human visits** | • 1960 – Jacques Piccard & Don Walsh (Trieste II). <br>• 2012 – James Cameron (Deepsea Challenger). <br>• 2019 – Victor Vescovo (Limiting Factor, crewed). <br>• 2020 – Uncrewed vehicle (DSSV *Pressure Drop*). |

So, if you’re looking for the ocean’s ultimate low point, it’s the Challenger Deep—still the deepest place on Earth’s surface that we have measured.
