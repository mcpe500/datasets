**Short answer:**  
In Irish folklore you’ll find a pot of gold guarded by a leprechaun. In reality a rainbow isn’t a physical object at all, so there’s nothing tangible waiting at its “end” – just a spot where the arc appears to meet the ground, which is usually a spray of water droplets.

---

## Why the “pot of gold” story endures  

- **Myth & metaphor:** The idea that a rainbow hides a treasure is a classic piece of Irish (and broader Celtic) folklore. It’s a way of saying that the most beautiful, fleeting things often have a hidden reward for those who pursue them.  
- **Cultural shorthand:** The image of a leprechaun and a gold‑filled cauldron is instantly recognizable, so writers, advertisers, and storytellers still use it when they want a quick symbol of “the prize at the end of a quest.”

---

## What a rainbow actually is  

| Feature | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Formation** | Sunlight enters a raindrop, refracts (bends), reflects off the back of the droplet, then refracts again as it exits. The different wavelengths (colors) bend at slightly different angles, spreading out into the familiar spectrum (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet). |
| **Shape** | From any given viewpoint the set of droplets that satisfy the exact 40–42° refraction angle forms a **circular arc** (or a full circle if you’re high enough, e.g., from an airplane). |
| **Location** | The arc is *relative* to you. Every observer sees a slightly different rainbow, because the angle is measured from your own eye. No two people share the exact same “end.” |
| **Physical existence** | The rainbow exists **only as light**; the water droplets themselves are ordinary rain, mist, or spray. There is no solid surface, no hidden vault, no magical pot. |

---

## What you’ll actually find if you try to reach the “end”

1. **A region of water droplets** – the very same misty spray that created the rainbow in the first place (e.g., a garden sprinkler, a waterfall, or a light rain).  
2. **A different perspective** – the rainbow you saw from the ground disappears; you now see the same droplets, but the specific angle that produced the colors is gone, so the colors vanish.  
3. **A new rainbow** – if you keep moving, the geometry of the light‑drop interaction changes, and you may see another rainbow elsewhere, never catching up to the original arc.

---

## The poetic take

Even though there’s no leprechaun’s gold, the phrase “the end of the rainbow” still works as a powerful metaphor:

- **Hope & aspiration:** The rainbow reminds us that after a storm there’s color and promise.  
- **Treasure hunts:** It encourages us to keep searching for something valuable—whether it’s a personal goal, a creative idea, or simply a moment of beauty.  

So while the literal answer is “just water droplets,” the mythic answer—*a pot of gold*—captures something deeper about why we’re drawn to the question in the first place.

---

### TL;DR

- **Folklore:** A pot of gold guarded by a leprechaun.  
- **Science:** No physical object; a rainbow is a perceptual arc of refracted light that moves with the observer’s viewpoint. The “end” is simply where the arc meets the ground—a place full of water spray, nothing more.  

If you chase it, you’ll end up with wet shoes and a fresh appreciation for the physics (and poetry) of light. 🌈🥇
