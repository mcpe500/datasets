The brightest star that can be seen in the night sky is **Sirius** (also called **Alpha Canis Majoris**).

- **Apparent magnitude**: ‑1.46 (the lower the number, the brighter the object).  
- **Constellation**: Canis Major (the “Greater Dog”), so it’s often called the “Dog Star.”  
- **Distance from Earth**: about 8.6 light‑years.  
- **Nature**: Sirius is actually a binary star system, consisting of the bright primary star Sirius A (a white‑main‑sequence star about twice the Sun’s mass) and a much fainter white‑dwarf companion, Sirius B.

*Note*: The Sun is, of course, the brightest star overall, but it isn’t visible at night. Among the stars that appear in the night sky, Sirius reigns supreme.
