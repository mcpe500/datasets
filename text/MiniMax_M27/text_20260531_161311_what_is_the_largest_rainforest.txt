The **Amazon Rainforest** (often simply called the Amazon) is the world’s largest rainforest. It spans roughly **5.5 million km²** (about 2.1 million sq mi) of primarily tropical forest across nine countries in South America—Brazil, Peru, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Bolivia, Guyana, Suriname, and French Guyana.

**Key facts**

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Amazon River basin, northern South America |
| **Total area (incl. non‑forest) | ~7 million km²** |
| **Forest area** | ~5.5 million km²** |
| **Biodiversity** | Home to ~10 % of all species on Earth; millions of plant, animal, and insect species |
| **Climate role** | Generates about 20 % of the world’s oxygen and acts as a massive carbon sink |
| **Notable wildlife** | Jaguar, Amazon river dolphin, sloths, toucans, anacondas, and countless insect species |

The Amazon’s sheer size and biological richness make it the planet’s most extensive and (by many measures) most important rainforest. The second‑largest is the **Congo Basin** in Central Africa, covering about 3.3 million km², but it remains considerably smaller than the Amazon.
