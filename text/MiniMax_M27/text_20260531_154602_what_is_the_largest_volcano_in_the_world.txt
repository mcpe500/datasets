**The biggest volcano we know of on Earth is Tamu Massif**, a massive underwater shield volcano in the western Pacific (east of Japan).  

| Property | Tamu Massif |
|----------|--------------|
| **Location** | Shatsky Rise, western Pacific Ocean (≈ 1,600 km east of Japan) |
| **Type** | Submarine shield volcano |
| **Area** | ≈ 120,000 km² (about the size of the British Isles) |
| **Volume** | ≈ 2.5 million km³ – far larger than any above‑sea‑level volcano |
| **Discovery** | First identified by seismic surveys in 2013; later confirmed by deep‑sea drilling |

Because it lies beneath ~2 km of ocean water, Tamu Massif is not visible from the surface, which is why many people are more familiar with the next entry.

---

### Largest volcano that rises above sea level  

If we restrict the answer to volcanoes that protrude above the ocean, the champion is **Mauna Loa** on the island of Hawai‘i.

| Property | Mauna Loa |
|----------|-----------|
| **Location** | Island of Hawai‘i, USA |
| **Type** | Shield volcano (active) |
| **Elevation** | 4,169 m (13,679 ft) above sea level |
| **Volume** | ≈ 40,000 km³ (≈ 9,600 cubic miles) |
| **Area** | ≈ 5,200 km² on the surface; the entire edifice extends well beyond the island’s shoreline |

Mauna Loa is the most massive volcano that we can actually see and climb. Its total volume, however, is only about **2 %** of Tamu Massif’s volume.

---

### In a broader context  

- **If we include all bodies in the Solar System**, the title goes to **Olympus Mons** on Mars, which stands 22 km high and spans ~ 600 km across, with a volume roughly 100 times larger than Mauna Loa.  
- **If you hear “largest volcano in the world” in everyday conversation**, most people are thinking of Mauna Loa because it’s the biggest volcano we can see from the surface.  

So:

- **Overall (including underwater volcanoes):** *Tamu Massif* is the largest.  
- **Largest above‑sea‑level volcano:** *Mauna Loa* in Hawai‘i.  

Both are shield volcanoes, built up by countless lava flows over millions of years.
