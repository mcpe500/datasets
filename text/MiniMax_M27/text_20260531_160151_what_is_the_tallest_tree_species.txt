**The tallest tree species on Earth is the coast redwood (*Sequoia sempervirens*)**, a conifer native to the fog‑laden coastal forests of northern California and southern Oregon.

### Why the coast redwood tops the list  

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Maximum recorded height** | ≈ 115 m – 117 m (≈ 380 ft – 385 ft). The tallest known individual is **Hyperion**, a coast redwood in Redwood National Park, California, measured at **115.92 m (380.3 ft)** in 2022. |
| **Typical height range** | 60 m – 90 m (200 ft – 300 ft) for mature trees, with occasional individuals surpassing 100 m (328 ft). |
| **Why it grows so tall** | • **Genetic potential**: Redwoods have a long evolutionary history of developing massive, columnar trunks.<br>• **Favorable climate**: Cool, moist coastal fog reduces water loss, allowing the trees to sustain a huge canopy.<br>• **Rich soils** on alluvial flats and steep slopes provide ample nutrients and water.<br>• **Fire adaptation**: Thick bark and the ability to sprout from basal burls help survive wildfires, preserving the long‑term growth potential. |

### Other notably tall species (for context)

| Species | Max. Height (≈) | Why it’s notable |
|--------|----------------|------------------|
| **Mountain ash** (*Eucalyptus regnans*) | ~ 100 m (328 ft) | The tallest **angiosperm** (flowering plant). Some historic individuals in Tasmania are reported near 115 m, though none have been measured as precisely as the redwoods. |
| **Giant sequoia** (*Sequoiadendron giganteum*) | 75 m – 85 m (250 ft – 280 ft) | Largest tree by volume, but not the tallest. |
| **Sitka spruce** (*Picea sitchensis*) | 90 m (295 ft) | One of the tallest conifers in the Pacific Northwest, especially in old‑growth stands. |
| **Kauri** (*Agathis australis*) | 70 m (230 ft) | Emergent in New Zealand lowland forests, though not as tall as the above. |
| **Dawn redwood** (*Metasequoia glyptostroboides*) | 60 m (200 ft) | A living fossil from China, relatively modest in height compared to its cousins. |

### Quick “fun facts”

- **Oldest known coast redwood**: The **Crocker** tree in Prairie Creek Redwoods State Park is estimated to be **2,000+ years old**. Age contributes to height, but redwoods can still grow hundreds of meters above the ground.
- **“Tallest tree” vs. “tallest organism”**: While the coast redwood holds the record for *individual* height, the **tallest living organism in terms of total length** is actually a **clonal colony** of the **“Pando” quaking aspen** (*Populus tremuloides*), which spreads via root systems but isn’t a single contiguous trunk. The question here focuses on distinct trees, so the redwood wins.
- **Measurement challenges**: Height is measured by climbing or using laser Rangefinders/LiDAR, and recent surveys using LiDAR have refined our estimates (e.g., the 2022 measurement of Hyperion).

### Bottom line
If you’re looking for the **tallest tree species in the world**, the answer is **coast redwood (*Sequoia sempervirens*)**, whose tallest known individuals exceed **115 m (380 ft)**. The record‑holder, **Hyperion**, stands at about **115.9 m (380 ft)**.
