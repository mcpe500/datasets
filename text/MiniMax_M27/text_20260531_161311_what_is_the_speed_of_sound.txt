The speed of sound is the distance that a sound wave travels per unit of time. In dry air at 20 °C (68 °F) the speed is about **343 meters per second (≈ 1,125 ft/s)**, which is roughly **1,235 km/h** or **767 mph**.

It varies with the medium and temperature:

| Medium | Typical temperature | Approx. speed |
|--------|---------------------|----------------|
| Air (sea‑level, 20 °C) | 20 °C | 343 m/s |
| Air (0 °C) | 0 °C | 331 m/s |
| Fresh water (20 °C) | 20 °C | ~1,480 m/s |
| Seawater (20 °C, average salinity) | 20 °C | ~1,530 m/s |
| Steel (solid) | room temp. | ~5,960 m/s |

**Key factors**

- **Temperature**: In gases, speed increases roughly with the square root of temperature (in Kelvin). For each degree Celsius increase, the speed in air rises about 0.6 m/s.
- **Medium**: Sound travels faster in liquids and solids than in gases because the particles are closer together, allowing quicker transmission of the pressure wave.
- **Humidity**: Humid air is less dense than dry air, so sound travels slightly faster in moist air (the effect is small, about 0.1 % at typical conditions).
- **Altitude/pressure**: At high altitudes the lower pressure reduces the speed slightly, but temperature effects dominate.

If you need the speed for a specific situation (e.g., a particular altitude or temperature), you can use the formula for an ideal gas:

\[
c = \sqrt{\gamma \cdot R \cdot T}
\]

where  
- \(c\) = speed of sound,  
- \(\gamma\) (gamma) = ratio of specific heats (≈ 1.40 for dry air),  
- \(R\) = specific gas constant (≈ 287 J/(kg·K) for air),  
- \(T\) = absolute temperature in Kelvin.
