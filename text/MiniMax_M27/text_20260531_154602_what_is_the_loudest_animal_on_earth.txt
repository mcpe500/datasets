**The sperm whale** holds the record for the loudest sound produced by any animal on Earth.

| Animal | Sound type | Measured source level (dB re 1 µPa at 1 m) | Typical frequency | How it’s used |
|--------|------------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Sperm whale** | Click (echolocation) | **≈ 230 dB** (some reports up to 236 dB) | 10 – 30 kHz (most energy) | Navigation, locating prey (e.g., giant squids) and communication over hundreds of kilometres |
| **Blue whale** | Low‑frequency “song” | ≈ 188 dB | 10–100 Hz | Long‑range communication across ocean basins |
| **Pistol (snapping) shrimp** | Snap bubble‑collapse | ≈ 200 dB (underwater) | 2–5 kHz | Stunning prey; extremely short‑duration sound |
| **Howler monkey** | Roar/call | ≈ 140 dB (air) | 0.5–4 kHz | Territory defence, social signalling |

### Why the sperm‑whale click is “the loudest”

1. **Intensity** – A sperm‑whale click can reach **≈ 230 dB** (decibels measured relative to 1 µPa at 1 m). For comparison, a jet engine at 30 m is about 140 dB, and the human pain threshold is around 130 dB in air. Because decibels are logarithmic, a 10‑dB increase represents a ten‑fold increase in intensity, so the sperm‑whale click is many orders of magnitude more intense than any terrestrial animal sound.

2. **Directional beamforming** – The click is generated by a specialized organ called the **phonic lip** in the whale’s forehead, then focused into a narrow, highly directional beam. This concentrates the acoustic energy into a small area, effectively making the source appear even louder in the direction the whale is pointing.

3. **Propagation distance** – The high‑intensity, short‑duration click can travel **hundreds of kilometres** in the deep ocean, allowing sperm whales to locate prey and communicate over vast distances.

### How it compares to other “loud” animals

- **Blue whales** produce the **lowest‑frequency, longest‑range calls** (up to ~188 dB), but their sounds are spread over a much broader frequency band and are not as intense as the sperm‑whale click.
- **Pistol shrimp** generate a **bubble‑collapse snap** that can reach ~200 dB underwater, but the sound is extremely brief (a few hundred microseconds) and the total acoustic energy is far lower than a sperm‑whale click.
- **Howler monkeys** are the loudest terrestrial mammals, yet their calls are only about 140 dB in air—roughly 100 dB less intense than a sperm‑whale click.

### Quick “fun fact”

A sperm‑whale click is so powerful that, if you were **1 meter** away in water, the pressure wave could cause temporary hearing damage or even a mild concussion. Fortunately, the whales usually emit these clicks several meters away from any potential listener, and the sound attenuates rapidly with distance.

---

**Bottom line:** While blue whales win the prize for the loudest *calls* heard across the entire ocean, the **sperm whale’s echolocation click** is the single loudest sound produced by any animal, reaching intensities of roughly 230 dB.
