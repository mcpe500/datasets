A rainbow isn’t an object that sits in the sky waiting for you to walk up to it. It’s a *phenomenon*—a trick of light caused by millions of tiny water droplets that act like tiny prisms and mirrors.

### How a rainbow works (the short version)

| Step | What happens |
|------|--------------|
| **Sunlight enters a droplet** | Light slows down and bends (refraction). |
| **Inside the droplet** | The light splits into its component colors (dispersion). |
| **Reflected off the back** | The colored light bounces back toward the front of the droplet. |
| **Exits the droplet** | It bends again (another refraction) and emerges at a specific angle—about **42°** for the red light and slightly smaller for the other colors. |

Because each droplet sends light to your eye at that fixed angle, the band of colors you see forms a **circular cone** centered on the line that runs from the sun, through your eye, and onward. From the ground we usually see only the upper half of that circle—an arc.

### Why you can’t “reach” the end

- **It follows your perspective.** As you move, the rainbow shifts so that the 42° angle is always centered on you. No matter how far you walk, the rainbow stays about the same distance away (roughly the distance from your eye to the “circle” of droplets that happen to be at the right angle).  
- **It’s not a solid surface.** There’s nothing solid to step on or dig up at any point on the arc. What you see are merely colors refracted from water droplets that are constantly drifting in the air.

### What you *might* find in stories and folklore

- **A pot of gold** guarded by a leprechaun. This is the classic Irish myth: if you can catch a leprechaun, you can claim his treasure—but the rainbow always moves before you get there, which is why the gold stays hidden.
- **A symbol of hope or promise.** Poets and songwriters often use “the end of the rainbow” as a metaphor for a long‑sought goal or a reward that appears after a storm.

### The bottom line

- **Scientifically:** There’s no physical “end” with a hidden object. The rainbow is a moving, perspective‑dependent circle of refracted light.
- **Culturally:** You’ll find the legendary pot of gold (or at least the idea of a treasure waiting just beyond reach) and all the hope that a rainbow has come to represent.

So, if you ever chase a rainbow, you’ll end up chasing a beautiful, fleeting light show and a lot of stories—nothing you can actually hold in your hands (unless you bring your own pot of gold).
