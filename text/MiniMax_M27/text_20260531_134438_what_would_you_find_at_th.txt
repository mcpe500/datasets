In folklore a rainbow is said to end in a hidden pot of gold guarded by a leprechaun (or, in some cultures, by a mischievous dwarf, a dragon, or some other mythical creature). If you could walk right up to that “spot,” you’d be rewarded with treasure.

From a scientific standpoint, however, a rainbow isn’t a solid object you can walk to—there’s no literal “end.” A rainbow is an optical phenomenon created when sunlight (or moonlight) is refracted, reflected, and dispersed inside countless raindrops. To any given observer, the rainbow appears as a circular arc (or a full circle when viewed from an aircraft) centred on the point directly opposite the sun (the anti‑solar point). Because the light is being refracted at a specific angle (~42° for a primary rainbow), the colors you see are always at that same angle from the anti‑solar point, so the “rainbow” moves with you—it never gets closer the way a road does.

So:

* **Mythic answer:** A glittering pot of gold, guarded by a leprechaun (or its cultural equivalent).
* **Physical reality:** A purely visual effect, with no tangible end you can reach—only a moving, circular band of colour that follows you as you change position.

If you’re ever lucky enough to spot a pot of gold at the foot of a rainbow, you’ll have discovered a classic bit of storytelling magic! Otherwise, you’ll find that the “end” of a rainbow is just a beautiful, fleeting play of light.
