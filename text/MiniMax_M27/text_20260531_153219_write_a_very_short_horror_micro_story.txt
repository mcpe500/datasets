The night was so silent the house seemed to hold its breath. Mara lit a candle and tiptoed to the basement door, the latch whispering as it opened. A draft brushed her neck, colder than the cellar’s stone, and she heard a faint, rhythmic tapping—like a child’s finger tapping on a glass. She turned, and in the flickering light she saw the silhouette of a small figure standing at the top of the stairs, smiling. The candle flickered out. In the darkness, the tapping grew louder, then stopped—followed by a soft, child‑like laugh, coming from everywhere at once.
