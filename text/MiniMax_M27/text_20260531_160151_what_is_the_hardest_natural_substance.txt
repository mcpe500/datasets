**Diamond** is the hardest natural material that occurs on Earth.

### Why diamond tops the list
| Property | Diamond |
|----------|---------|
| **Mohs hardness** | 10 (the maximum on the scale) |
| **Vickers hardness** | 70–100 GPa (≈ 7,000–10,000 kgf mm⁻²) |
| **Bulk modulus** | ~440 GPa (very stiff) |
| **Composition** | Pure carbon in a tetrahedral covalent network |

- **Mohs scale** (1–10) ranks minerals by whether they can scratch each other. Diamond’s rating of 10 means it can scratch every other natural mineral.
- **Vickers hardness** measures resistance to deformation under a pointed indenter. Diamond’s 70–100 GPa is far higher than any other natural material (e.g., quartz ≈ 9 GPa, topaz ≈ 2 GPa).

### A close natural runner‑up: Lonsdaleite
- **Lonsdaleite** is a hexagonal (wurtzite‑type) form of diamond found in some meteorite impact structures (e.g., the Canyon Diablo crater, the Sudbury basin).  
- Theoretical studies suggest it could be *harder* than ordinary cubic diamond under certain stress conditions, but its natural occurrence is sparse and it is usually intergrown with cubic diamond, making reliable hardness measurements difficult.  

### What “hardest” means
Hardness can be measured in several ways:
- **Scratch resistance** → Mohs scale (qualitative).
- **Indentation resistance** → Vickers, Knoop, or Brinell hardness (quantitative, in GPa or MPa).

Because diamond dominates both the qualitative and quantitative rankings, it’s universally accepted as the hardest natural substance.

### Quick reference
| Natural Substance | Mohs | Vickers (GPa) | Remarks |
|-------------------|------|--------------|---------|
| **Diamond** | 10 | 70–100 | Found in kimberlite pipes, alluvial deposits |
| **Lonsdaleite** | ≈10 (theoretical) | ~100+ (theoretical) | Rare, meteorite‑derived |
| **Corundum** (e.g., ruby, sapphire) | 9 | ~20 | Hard, but not as hard as diamond |
| **Topaz** | 8 | ~6–7 | Transparent, used in jewelry |
| **Quartz** | 7 | ~9 | Common in sand and many rocks |

### Bottom line
If you need the toughest material that nature provides, reach for **diamond**—whether it’s a gem in a ring or a industrial cutting tool, it stands at the top of the hardness hierarchy.
