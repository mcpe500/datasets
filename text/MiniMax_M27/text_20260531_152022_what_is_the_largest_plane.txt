The largest planet in our solar system is **Jupiter**.

- **Size**: With a mean radius of about 69,911 km (about 11 times the Earth’s radius), Jupiter is the biggest planet.
- **Mass**: Its mass is roughly 1.90 × 10²⁷ kg, which is more than twice the combined mass of all the other planets, moons, asteroids, and comets in the solar system.
- **Type**: It’s a gas giant, composed mainly of hydrogen and helium, with no solid surface.
- **Notable features**: The Great Red Spot (a massive storm), a faint ring system, and at least 95 known moons—including the four large Galilean moons (Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto).

So, if you’re looking for the “biggest” world orbiting the Sun, that’s Jupiter.
