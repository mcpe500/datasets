**Short answer:** The animal that produces the loudest *measurable* sound on the planet is the **sperm whale**. Its clicks can exceed **230 dB SPL (re 1 µPa at 1 m)** in the water, which is far above any other creature.

---

### Why the sperm whale wins

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Sound type** | Short, broadband “clicks” used for echolocation and communication. |
| **Maximum source level** | ~230 dB re 1 µPa · m (underwater). |
| **How it’s measured** | Hydrophones placed a few meters from the animal; the pressure peak of the click is recorded and expressed in decibels relative to a reference pressure in water. |
| **Why it’s so loud** | The click is generated by a specialized organ (the “phonic lips”) that forces a sudden, massive exchange of air between nasal sacs. The sound is focused into a narrow beam, concentrating the acoustic energy. |
| **Converted to air‑borne SPL** | If that same energy were transmitted into air, it would be on the order of **170 dB SPL**, still louder than any terrestrial animal. |

Because decibels are logarithmic, a 10 dB increase represents a ten‑fold increase in acoustic power, and a 20 dB increase represents a hundred‑fold increase. So 230 dB is astronomically more intense than the 140 dB of a howler monkey or the 120 dB of a screaming cicada.

---

### Other “loud” contenders (for context)

| Environment | Animal | Approx. Max SPL (dB) | Typical use |
|-------------|--------|-----------------------|-------------|
| **Underwater (air‑filled mammals)** | **Blue whale** | ~188 dB re 1 µPa · m | Long‑range, low‑frequency calls for mating and social coordination. |
| **Underwater (other cetaceans)** | **Humpback whale** | ~180 dB | Songs, especially during breeding seasons. |
| **Land (air)** | **Howler monkey** (Alouatta) | ~140 dB SPL at source | Territorial calls. |
| **Land (air)** | **White‑bellied piha** (Lipaugus vociferans) | ~125 dB SPL at 1 m | Mating calls. |
| **Land (air)** | **Common cicada** (e.g., Magicicada) | ~120 dB SPL at close range | Mate attraction. |
| **Land (air)** | **Pistol (snapping) shrimp** (Alpheus) | ~200 dB **underwater**; negligible in air. | Cavitation bubble snap for hunting. |
| **Air (birds)** | **White‑bellied bird of paradise** (Semioptera wallacii) | ~125 dB SPL at 1 m | Courtship display. |

> **Note:** In the **air** (the medium we humans usually experience), the loudest animal is the **howler monkey**, whose roars can be heard up to 5 km away. The **white‑bellied piha** and the **white‑bellied bird of paradise** are the champion birds, while cicadas claim the insect record.

---

### Quick guide to “loudness” terminology

| Term | Definition | Typical reference |
|------|------------|-------------------|
| **dB SPL** (Sound Pressure Level) | 20 × log10(p/p₀), where p₀ = 20 µPa (air) or 1 µPa (water). | Human conversation ≈ 60 dB SPL (air). |
| **Source level** | dB SPL measured 1 m from the animal’s mouth/sound‑producing organ. | Allows comparison across species. |
| **Propagation loss** | Sound weakens with distance; underwater it’s roughly 10 log10(r) for spherical spreading. | Determines how far a sound can be heard. |

---

### TL;DR

- **Overall loudest animal:** **Sperm whale** (≈ 230 dB SPL underwater).  
- **Loudest land animal:** **Howler monkey** (≈ 140 dB SPL in air).  
- **Loudest bird:** **White‑bellied piha** (≈ 125 dB SPL).  
- **Loudest insect:** **Cicada** (≈ 120 dB SPL).  

These values highlight just how powerful acoustic communication can be—especially in the ocean, where sound travels far more efficiently than in air.
