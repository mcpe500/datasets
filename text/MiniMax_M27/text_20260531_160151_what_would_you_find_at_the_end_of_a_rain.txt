A rainbow isn’t a solid object that has a “place” you can walk up to, so there’s no physical “thing” waiting at its end. What you’ll find depends on whether you’re thinking about the science or the folklore:

### The scientific view
- **A rainbow is an optical illusion.** It’s produced when sunlight (or, more rarely, moonlight) is refracted, reflected, and dispersed by countless droplets of water in the atmosphere.  
- **The “rainbow” you see is actually a full circle** (or a segment of one) centered on the line that connects your eye to the antisolar point (the point directly opposite the sun).  
- Because the rainbow is defined by your own line of sight, each observer sees a different rainbow that follows them as they move. You can never actually reach the “end” of a rainbow; it recedes as you approach it.  

So, scientifically, the “end of a rainbow” is simply more of the same light‑spectrum pattern, and no treasure or hidden object lies there.

### The folklore and myth
- In many cultures the idea of a **pot of gold** hidden at the foot of a rainbow has become a classic symbol.  
- The story usually involves a **leprechaun** or other mischievous sprite guarding that gold, rewarding the lucky finder (or punishing the greedy).  
- This legend is a playful way to capture the idea that rainbows seem to promise something wonderful just out of reach.

### Bottom line
- **Real‑world answer:** Nothing physical—rainbows are light, not objects.  
- **Story‑book answer:** A pot of gold guarded by a leprechaun (or whatever whimsical guardian the culture prefers).  

Either way, the rainbow remains a beautiful, fleeting reminder that some things are best enjoyed for what they are rather than chased for what they might contain.
