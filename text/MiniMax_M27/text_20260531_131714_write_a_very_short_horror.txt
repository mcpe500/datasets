The last thing the old lighthouse keeper remembered was the creak of the staircase. He stepped outside, the fog swallowing his lantern, and heard a soft, child‑like humming behind the glass. He turned; the beam of light cut through the mist and revealed a pale face pressed against the pane, eyes black, mouth moving silently. He reached for the handle, but the door was already unlocked. Inside, the air was cold and smelled of brine, and the humming grew louder, as if something just out of sight was pacing the narrow hallway. He whispered, “Who’s there?” The humming stopped. Silence flooded the chamber, broken only by the faint, rhythmic tap of footsteps that seemed to come from everywhere—and nowhere—until they stopped just behind him, and a breath brushed his neck. He didn’t turn around. He didn’t need to. The light outside flickered once, then went dark.
