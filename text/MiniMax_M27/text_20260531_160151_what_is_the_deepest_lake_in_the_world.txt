The deepest lake in the world is **Lake Baikal** in Siberia, Russia.

Here are some key facts about Lake Baikal:

*   **Maximum Depth:** 1,642 meters (5,387 feet).
*   **Why it's so deep:** It sits in an active continental rift zone, where the Earth's crust is pulling apart, allowing the basin to deepen over time.
*   **Volume & Age:** It holds about 20% of the world's unfrozen fresh surface water and is estimated to be around 25–30 million years old, making it the world's oldest lake.
*   **Unique Ecosystem:** It's home to thousands of endemic species (plants and animals found nowhere else), including the freshwater seal, the Baikal seal.

For comparison, the next deepest lakes are:
1.  **Lake Tanganyika** (East Africa) - 1,470 m (4,823 ft)
2.  **Caspian Sea** (but technically a lake) - 1,025 m (3,363 ft)
3.  **Lake Vostok** (Antarctica, subglacial) - ~900+ m (2,953 ft)
4.  **Lake McKenzie (or Crater Lake, USA)** - 594 m (1,949 ft)
