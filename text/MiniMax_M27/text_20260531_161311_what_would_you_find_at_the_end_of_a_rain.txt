At the end of a rainbow you’d find a mixture of science, myth, and a little bit of imagination.

**The scientific truth**  
A rainbow isn’t an object that sits on the horizon—it’s an optical phenomenon that forms when sunlight is refracted, reflected, and dispersed by countless water droplets in the atmosphere. Because the light is bent at a specific angle (about 42°), each observer sees a slightly different rainbow that is always centered on the line from the sun through their own eyes. As you move toward what looks like the “end” of the bow, the rainbow shifts, so the apparent endpoint is always just out of reach.

**Folklore and legend**  
In many cultures the rainbow is said to hide treasure. The most famous story is the Irish legend of the pot of gold that a leprechaun buries at the exact point where a rainbow touches the ground. If you manage to catch the leprechaun, you can claim the gold—but, of course, the mischievous leprechaun usually escapes by vanishing along with the rainbow.

**A “glory” instead of a pot of gold**  
If you look at the sky from an airplane or a high mountain and the sun is low behind you, you can sometimes see a circular “glory”—a series of concentric, colorful rings—centered on the shadow of your own aircraft or yourself. In that sense, the “end” of the rainbow becomes an even more ethereal halo of light rather than a bucket of coins.

**Take‑away**  
- **Physically:** There is no fixed “end” you can walk to; each rainbow moves with you and is a personal optical illusion.
- **Mythologically:** The classic answer is the legendary pot of gold guarded by a leprechaun.
- **Visually:** The rainbow’s true shape is a circle (or a full disc, partially hidden by the ground), so the “end” is simply the point where your line of sight meets the circular arc.

So, if you ever chase a rainbow, you’ll end up discovering that the real treasure is the fleeting beauty of the phenomenon itself—and maybe a good story to tell about that mischievous leprechaun!
