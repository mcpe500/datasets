The deepest known ocean trench on Earth is the **Mariana Trench**, located in the western Pacific Ocean east of the Mariana Islands.  

- **Deepest point:** The trench’s lowest point is called **Challenger Deep**, which has been measured at roughly **10,935 m (35,876 ft)** (more recent surveys give an official depth of about **10,984 m (36,037 ft)**).  
- **Length & width:** The trench extends about **2,500 km (1,550 mi)** in a crescent shape, with a width of roughly **70 km (44 mi)**.  
- **Geologic origin:** It formed where the **Pacific Plate** is being subducted beneath the **Philippine Plate**, creating an extremely deep oceanic trench.  

Exploration milestones  
- **1960:** Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh made the first crewed descent to Challenger Deep in the bathyscaphe *Trieste*.  
- **2012:** Filmmaker James Cameron solo‑piloted the deep‑sea submersible *Deepsea Challenger* to the bottom.  
- **2020‑2021:** Chinese and other research teams have sent uncrewed and crewed vehicles (e.g., *Fendouzhe*) to the trench, gathering scientific data.

While other trenches like the **Tonga Trench** (second deepest at ~10,800 m) also descend deeply, the Mariana Trench holds the record for the deepest point on the planet.
