The **sailfish** (Istiophorus platypterus) is widely regarded as the fastest sea creature.  It can reach burst speeds of **≈ 68 mph (≈ 110 km/h)**, and in short sprints it has been recorded at up to **80 mph (≈ 130 km/h)**.

**Why it’s so fast**

- **Streamlined body** – a long, slender, laterally compressed shape that cuts through water with minimal drag.
- **Large, crescent‑shaped caudal fin** – provides powerful thrust for rapid acceleration.
- **High muscle mass** – a large proportion of red muscle fibers that generate sustained power.
- **Behavioral use** – sailfish use their speed to herd and strike schools of smaller fish, often coordinating with other sailfish to create “bait balls.”

**Other notably fast sea animals (for comparison)**  

| Animal | Approx. top speed | Typical measurement |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Black marlin** (Makaira indica) | ~80 mph (≈ 130 km/h) | Burst sprint |
| **Swordfish** (Xiphias gladius) | ~60 mph (≈ 97 km/h) | Sustained cruising |
| **Mako shark** (Isurus spp.) | ~45–50 mph (≈ 72–80 km/h) | Sustained swimming |
| **Orca** (Orcinus orca) | ~34 mph (≈ 55 km/h) | Short bursts |
| **Common dolphin** (Delphinus delphis) | ~30 mph (≈ 48 km/h) | Sustained swimming |

So, while a few other large pelagic fish can rival the sailfish’s burst speed, the sailfish remains the most consistently cited “fastest” sea creature.
