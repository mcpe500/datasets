The **smallest country in the world** by both **area** and **population** is **Vatican City** (Status Civitatis Vaticanae).

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Area** | 0.44 km² (about 44 hectares) |
| **Population** | ~800 people (2023 estimate) |
| **Location** | Within Rome, Italy |
| **Government** | Theocratic elective monarchy (the Pope) |
| **Founded** | 1929 (Lateran Treaty) |
| **Official languages** | Latin (official), Italian (working) |

### Why it’s considered the smallest

- **Area**: Vatican City covers just 0.44 km², making it the tiniest sovereign state on Earth. The next‑smallest countries by area are Monaco (2.02 km²), Nauru (21 km²), and Tuvalu (26 km²).
- **Population**: With only about 800 residents (mostly clergy, members of the Swiss Guard, and a few lay workers), it also has the smallest permanent population of any independent nation.

### Notable facts

- **Enclaved**: It is entirely surrounded by the city of Rome, giving it a unique “city‑within‑a‑city” status.
- **International status**: Despite its tiny size, Vatican City is a full member of several international organizations (e.g., UNESCO, UN observer state) and issues its own passports, stamps, and currency.
- **Historical significance**: It houses St. Peter’s Basilica, the Vatican Museums, and the Sistine Chapel—major centers of art, culture, and Catholic heritage.
