The brightest star visible in the night sky is **Sirius** (also called **α Canis Majoris**).  

- **Apparent magnitude:** about –1.46, making it the most luminous star we can see without a telescope.  
- **Distance:** roughly 8.6 light‑years from Earth.  
- **Type:** Sirius A is a white main‑sequence star (spectral type A1 V) with a faint white‑dwarf companion, Sirius B.  

(Of course, the Sun is far brighter, but it isn’t part of the “night sky.”)
