**The speed of sound is the distance a sound wave travels per unit time.**  
It depends on the medium the wave is moving through (air, water, steel, etc.) and on conditions such as temperature (and, to a much smaller extent, humidity or pressure).

---

## 1. In dry air at sea‑level pressure  

| Temperature (°C) | Speed of sound (m s⁻¹) | Speed (km h⁻¹) | Speed (mph) |
|------------------|------------------------|---------------|------------|
| **0**            | **331 m/s**            | **1 193 km/h** | **740 mph** |
| **20** (room temp) | **≈ 343 m/s**      | **≈ 1 235 km/h** | **≈ 767 mph** |
| **30**            | **≈ 349 m/s**          | **≈ 1 256 km/h** | **≈ 780 mph** |

A handy rule of thumb for dry air:  

\[
v \approx 331\;+\;0.6\,T_{\!^\circ\!C}\; \text{m/s}
\]

So the speed rises by about **0.6 m/s for each degree Celsius increase** in temperature.

> **Humidity** has only a tiny effect: at 20 °C, a 50 % relative‑humidity increase raises the speed by ≈ 0.1 m/s, so it’s usually ignored for most practical purposes.

---

## 2. In other common media  

| Medium | Approx. speed of sound | Typical value |
|--------|------------------------|---------------|
| **Fresh water** (20 °C) | 1,480 m/s | ≈ 5,328 km/h |
| **Seawater** (20 °C, typical salinity) | 1,520 m/s | ≈ 5,472 km/h |
| **Ice** (‑10 °C) | ≈ 3,200 m/s | ≈ 11,520 km/h |
| **Wood (soft pine)** | ≈ 3,300 m/s | ≈ 11,880 km/h |
| **Aluminium** | ≈ 6,300 m/s | ≈ 22,680 km/h |
| **Steel** | ≈ 5,900–6,100 m/s | ≈ 21,240–21,960 km/h |
| **Rubber** (soft) | ≈ 40–60 m/s | ≈ 144–216 km/h |

These numbers vary with material density, elastic modulus, and temperature, but they give a quick sense of how dramatically the speed can change from one medium to another.

---

## 3. Why does it change with temperature (in gases)?

For an ideal gas, the speed of sound is:

\[
v = \sqrt{\gamma\,R\,T / M}
\]

- **γ (gamma)** = ratio of specific heats (≈ 1.40 for diatomic gases like air).  
- **R** = universal gas constant (8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹).  
- **T** = absolute temperature (K).  
- **M** = molar mass of the gas (≈ 0.02896 kg mol⁻¹ for dry air).

Plugging in standard sea‑level pressure and a temperature of 20 °C (293 K) yields ≈ 343 m/s, matching the measured value.

---

## 4. Quick reference “Mach” numbers

- **Mach 1** = speed of sound in the surrounding medium.  
- At 20 °C in air, Mach 1 ≈ 343 m/s (≈ 1,235 km/h).  
- A jet traveling at Mach 2 would be moving roughly twice that speed (≈ 2,470 km/h).

---

### Bottom line
- **In everyday dry air at room temperature (≈ 20 °C): ~343 m/s (≈ 1,235 km/h, ≈ 767 mph).**  
- **The speed rises about 0.6 m/s for each degree Celsius you heat the air.**  
- **In water, the sound travels roughly 4–5 times faster than in air; in metals, it’s even faster.**
