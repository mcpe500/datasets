The speed of light in a vacuum is a fundamental constant of nature. Its exact value is

\[
c = 299\,792\,458\ \text{meters per second}
\]

Because the meter is defined by the distance light travels in a given time, this value is exact by definition.

**Typical rounded forms**

- **≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s** (often used in everyday calculations)  
- **≈ 1.079 × 10⁹ km/h**  
- **≈ 670,616,629 mph**  

In other media (e.g., air, water, glass) light travels more slowly; for example, in water it’s about 225,000 km/s (≈ 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s). But the quoted “speed of light” usually refers to the vacuum value.
