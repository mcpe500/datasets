The largest mammal – and indeed the largest animal known to have ever existed – is the **blue whale** (*Balaenoptera musculus*).

| Feature | Approximate measurement |
|---------|--------------------------|
| **Length** | Up to about 30 m (≈ 98 ft); most adults are 24–27 m (80–90 ft) |
| **Weight** | Up to roughly 150–200 metric tons (≈ 330,000–440,000 lb); the heaviest recorded was about 199 t |
| **Heart size** | About the size of a small car (~200 kg) |
| **Daily food intake** | Up to 4 tons of krill during peak feeding season |

Blue whales are marine mammals, meaning they breathe air, give live birth, and nurse their calves with milk. Despite their enormous size, they feed almost exclusively on tiny crustaceans called krill, straining them from the water with baleen plates in their mouths.

**Why they’re the biggest**

- **Evolutionary pressures**: In the ocean, buoyancy supports large body masses, allowing whales to grow far larger than any land mammal.
- **High-energy diet**: Dense swarms of krill provide enough calories to sustain such massive bodies.
- **Thermoregulation**: Their thick blubber layer and large body volume help retain heat, a crucial adaptation for life in cold seas.

**Other large mammals for context**

| Mammal | Approx. max length | Approx. max weight |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Fin whale (*Balaenoptera physalus*) | ~27 m (88 ft) | ~70 t |
| Sperm whale (*Physeter macrocephalus*) | ~18 m (59 ft) | ~50 t |
| African elephant (*Loxodonta africana*) | ~7 m (23 ft) (body length) | ~6–7 t |
| Giraffe (*Giraffa camelopardalis*) | ~5–6 m (16–20 ft) (height) | ~1.2 t |

So, when someone asks “What is the largest mammal?” the answer is unequivocally the **blue whale**.
